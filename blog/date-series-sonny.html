Bites Date #5: Sonny the Cuckoo Bird
📋 THE DATE FILE
The Suitor
Sonny — funny, enthusiastic, emotionally available, cuckoo for one specific thing
The Venue
A retro diner. Chrome stools, neon signs, milkshakes that should require permits.
Red Flags
Flowers chosen to resemble Cocoa Puffs. Every topic became a Cocoa Puffs story.
Green Flags
Arrived early. Paid. Walked me to my car. Texted to check I got home safe.
Official Ranking
3rd (exactly right)
Guests at the Table
Me. Sonny. And Cocoa Puffs.

Going into this date, I felt pretty good about my chances. Sonny had finished third in our mascot rankings for a reason. He was funny, enthusiastic, emotionally available, and seemed genuinely happy to be alive, which already put him ahead of several mascots I had encountered previously. I spent far longer than I should have deciding what to wear, eventually settling on my favorite green hoodie, black joggers, and sneakers. I was aiming for casual but cute. Looking back, I should have dressed for the situation I was actually walking into, which was apparently a long term committed relationship between a bird and a breakfast cereal.

The date started great. Sonny picked a retro diner with chrome stools, neon signs, and milkshakes so large they should have required permits. He arrived early, brought flowers, and immediately made me feel like the most important person in the room. The flowers were brown and yellow because they reminded him of Cocoa Puffs. At the time, I thought that was quirky. In hindsight, it was probably the equivalent of showing up to a first date carrying photos of your ex. Still, he was charming. He listened. He laughed at my jokes. He remembered things I'd mentioned weeks earlier. For the first half hour, I found myself wondering if we had completely underrated him.

Then I made the mistake of asking what he'd been up to lately.

Reader, I have never seen someone answer a simple question with so much cereal.

Apparently Sonny had recently heard about a promotional Cocoa Puffs display at a grocery store forty five minutes away. Most people would think, "That's neat." Sonny treated it like the final chapter of an adventure novel. There were setbacks. There was traffic. There was uncertainty. There was a moment where he genuinely thought the display had already been taken down. By the time he reached the ending, I felt emotionally invested in a cardboard cereal display and deeply confused about how I got there.

As the evening continued, I realized every conversation eventually found its way back to Cocoa Puffs. It didn't matter where we started. Childhood memories became Cocoa Puffs stories. Travel stories became Cocoa Puffs stories. Life lessons somehow became Cocoa Puffs stories. At one point I asked about his greatest accomplishment, expecting something meaningful. Instead, I got a detailed account of a box he once found that had an unusually high concentration of chocolate coating. The man spoke about that cereal box with the pride most people reserve for graduations and military promotions.

The moment everything finally clicked happened when the waitress casually mentioned that the diner offered a seasonal Cocoa Puffs milkshake. I have never witnessed a more dramatic emotional response to menu information. Sonny froze. Not in a funny way. In a genuine way. His eyes widened. His posture changed. He started asking questions with the intensity of a reporter investigating a major scandal. How long had it been on the menu? Was it seasonal? Had the recipe changed? Was it available year round? The waitress looked increasingly concerned while I sat there realizing I was watching someone reunite with the love of his life.

When the milkshake finally arrived, things somehow got worse. Sonny took pictures. Multiple pictures. From different angles. Then he stared at it silently for several seconds. Not talking. Not moving. Just staring. I was sitting across from him in my carefully chosen date outfit watching what can only be described as engagement photos for a dessert. Then he took a sip and achieved a level of happiness I didn't previously know was possible. Every problem disappeared from his face. Every concern vanished. If inner peace exists, I'm fairly certain I watched someone find it in a chocolate flavored milkshake.

The frustrating part is that he remained an excellent date the entire time. He paid for dinner. He walked me to my car. He texted afterward to make sure I got home safely. Unfortunately, he also sent three additional texts about the milkshake. When we eventually published our rankings and placed Sonny third, some people thought he should have been lower. Others thought he should have been higher. Personally, after reviewing the evidence, I think third is exactly right. Sonny is loyal, passionate, funny, thoughtful, and completely authentic. The problem is that every relationship requires two participants, and all evening I had the distinct feeling there were three of us sitting at that table.

Me. Sonny. And Cocoa Puffs.
