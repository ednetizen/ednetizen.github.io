Bites Date #4: Snap, Crackle & Pop
📋 THE DATE FILE
The Suitor(s)
Snap, Crackle & Pop — never fewer than three of them
The Venue
14 minutes from all participants. 4.8 stars. Awarded like a government contract.
Red Flags
A printed color spreadsheet with tabs. A prior site visit. "Date resilience."
Green Flags
Alarmingly healthy communication. Real-time conflict resolution. Nobody interrupts.
Official Ranking
4th (a surprisingly strong showing)
You Are Dating
Governance.

When we published our mascot rankings, several people questioned whether Snap, Crackle, and Pop should really count as a single entry. After spending an evening with them, I can confidently say they absolutely should.

Because they certainly do.

The restaurant selection should have warned me. Most people choose a restaurant because the food looks good or because a friend recommended it. Snap, Crackle, and Pop apparently assembled a planning committee. The restaurant was exactly fourteen minutes from all participants, had a 4.8 star rating, multiple parking options, public transportation access, indoor and outdoor seating, gluten free accommodations, vegetarian accommodations, and what Snap repeatedly referred to as "strong operational flexibility." Nothing about the location was romantic. Nothing about it was spontaneous. It felt less like they had chosen a date spot and more like they had awarded a government contract.

When I complimented the choice, Snap handed me a spreadsheet. Not showed me a spreadsheet. Handed me one. Printed. In color. There were tabs.

The spreadsheet compared fourteen restaurants across dozens of categories. Travel time. Parking availability. Menu diversity. Noise levels. Dessert quality. Weather contingencies. Seating arrangements. Something called "date resilience."

I never learned what date resilience meant because I became frightened and changed the subject. The truly alarming part was that they weren't even trying to impress me. They genuinely believed this was normal behavior. At one point Pop proudly explained they had conducted a site visit three weeks earlier. Crackle looked embarrassed. Snap looked proud. I started wondering if I needed legal representation.

The weird thing is that they are genuinely different people. Snap is clearly the organized one. Within fifteen minutes he had established an itinerary, a backup itinerary, and what appeared to be a process for collecting lessons learned afterward. Pop possessed the dangerous confidence of someone who has never experienced consequences and wasn't planning to start that evening. Crackle spent most of the night trying his best and looking slightly overwhelmed by the fact that his two best friends had apparently turned dinner into a strategic planning exercise.

Individually, all three were delightful. Collectively, they were deeply unsettling.

Every question somehow became a committee meeting. I would ask something simple, like where they wanted to travel someday, and immediately watch them begin consultations. Snap would discuss logistics. Pop would discuss excitement. Crackle would suggest something perfectly reasonable that everyone ignored. Several minutes later they would return with a unified position, complete with supporting rationale and implementation considerations. I wasn't participating in conversations anymore. I was receiving official statements.

The truly horrifying part was how functional they were. Going into the evening, I expected chaos. Instead, I discovered one of the healthiest relationships I have ever witnessed. They communicate. They support each other. They respect boundaries. Nobody interrupts. Nobody gets defensive. Nobody passive aggressively brings up something from six months ago. Every disagreement is resolved in real time. Every problem gets addressed immediately. At one point I realized I wasn't on a date. I was being shown up by three tiny cereal mascots who had somehow mastered interpersonal relationships.

The breaking point came when the waiter accidentally brought the wrong appetizer. Reader, this should have created a problem. It didn't. Within seconds Snap had identified the issue, Pop had reassured the waiter that everything was fine, and Crackle had proposed a solution. The entire situation was resolved so efficiently that nearby tables actually started watching. I genuinely believe those three could successfully negotiate a hostage situation.

As the evening continued, I slowly realized I wasn't on a date with three mascots. I was attending a board meeting. Every opinion required consensus. Every decision required discussion. Every joke somehow received stakeholder feedback. At one point Pop referred to the group as "we" seventeen consecutive times. I honestly don't know if they're best friends, business partners, or some sort of highly functioning breakfast collective.

By dessert, I was no longer evaluating romantic compatibility. I was evaluating whether I had accidentally joined a startup. Nobody flirted. Nobody made a move. Instead, I received a detailed explanation of their communication process and conflict resolution framework. At one point I became genuinely concerned they were preparing to offer me a benefits package.

Months later, when we ranked them fourth, some readers argued they deserved to be higher. Others argued they shouldn't count because they're technically three mascots. After careful consideration, I believe both groups are wrong. The issue isn't that there are three of them. The issue is that there are never fewer than three of them. You are not dating Snap. You are not dating Crackle. You are not dating Pop.

You are dating governance.

I didn't leave feeling rejected. I left feeling like I had failed the interview. And based on the professionalism of the follow up email, I'm fairly certain they held a debrief about me afterward.
